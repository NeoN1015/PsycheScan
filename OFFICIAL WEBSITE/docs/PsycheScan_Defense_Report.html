Background of the Study

From subjective questionnaires to signals the body gives off

Stress and anxiety affect over 260 million people globally, according to the World Health Organization, and are leading contributors to disability. For health practitioners, early and accurate detection is critical for timely intervention. (Brasier et al., 2025)

THE LIMIT

Assessments miss real-time change

Clinical interviews and questionnaires suffer from subjectivity and recall bias, and can't capture how a patient's state changes in the moment.

THE SIGNALS

Vital signs track stress

Heart rate variability, electrodermal activity, respiration and body temperature correlate strongly with stress and anxiety, and can be measured continuously. (Kargarandehkordi et al., 2025)

THE MODEL

CNN finds patterns, LSTM follows time

CNNs pick out local, spatial patterns; LSTMs capture long-term dependencies. Together they detect subtle changes in mental state. (Tanwar et al., 2022)

THE EVIDENCE
98.6% · 95%

EmotionNet, EEG with CNN-LSTM (Daadaa, 2025); AI wearable mental-health system with CNN and LSTM (Mouthami et al., 2025).

THE GAP

Not yet in clinical workflows

Data standardization, interpretability and clinical integration remain open problems (Xing et al., 2025). This study builds a real-time early detection system to help practitioners act sooner.

Review of Related Literature

Six threads the literature agrees on

Rising youth distress, weak self-report and cloud wearables, and maturing embedded and deep-learning tools point to the same answer: a locally processed, multimodal, CNN-LSTM screening platform.

Need

Mental health screening and youth vulnerability

  • Over 1.5M Filipino youth affected; worsened by academic, economic and pandemic stress (Marquez et al., 2025).
  • DOH (2023) calls for scalable, community-based screening.
  • PHQ-9 relies on self-report and verbal disclosure, limiting it in low-literacy or stigmatized settings (Bautista & Reyes, 2022).
Feasibility

Wearables for real-time stress monitoring

  • AI smart wristband detects stress on-device, offline (Mitro et al., 2024).
  • Smart Band AI system tracks stress, fatigue and anxiety from HR, temperature and movement (Bai et al., 2025).
  • MindBand: affordable, offline AI for underserved populations (Satapathy et al.).
  • WESAD: multimodal fusion beats single sensors (Schmidt et al., 2018).
Gap

Limits of commercial wearables

  • Fitbit Sense, Oura Ring and Apple Watch Series 9 use proprietary, cloud-based algorithms (Jolly et al., 2025; Delos Reyes & Tan, 2024).
  • They lack multimodal integration for diagnostic-grade analysis (Hickey et al., 2021).
  • Most aren't validated against clinical standards (Sharma, 2025).
Hardware

Multimodal sensors on embedded platforms

  • HW-827, GSR, DS18B20 and MPU6050 already proven in real-time monitoring (Ahmed & Kumar, 2023).
  • Facial landmarks plus biometrics improve stress detection (Hosseini et al., 2023).
  • Offline embedded systems suit rural screening, supporting ESP32 (Lee & Park, 2022).
Model

Deep learning for emotion: CNN-LSTM

  • Over 90% stress classification with CNN-LSTM (Chen & Zhang, 2021; Tanwar et al., 2022).
  • Robust in real time (Jain & Srihari, 2024).
  • CNN catches stress-related microexpressions (Kishore et al., 2025).
  • Face + physiology beats either alone (Wang et al., 2020).
Use

Decision support in community health

  • AI decision support adds objective, data-driven assessment (Tutun et al., 2023).
  • Barangay health workers need digital screening tools (Gonzales & Lim, 2023).
  • Offline, localized tools bridge research and practice in Southeast Asia (Alvarez & Santos, 2024).

Theoretical framework

Picard, 1997

Affective computing

Machines can recognize emotion from physiological and behavioral cues. This guided combining facial expression analysis with biometric sensing.

Hans Selye

General Adaptation Syndrome

AlarmSympathetic activation: heart rate and EDA rise
ResistanceResponses stay elevated
ExhaustionHigher risk of anxiety

Links stress to measurable changes in heart rate, skin conductance and temperature, which justifies the sensors.

Lazarus & Folkman, 1984

Transactional Model of Stress and Coping

Primary appraisalIs this a threat?
Secondary appraisalCan I cope?

Stress depends on perception, which shows on the face. This justifies pairing facial expression with physiology.

Chen & Zhang, 2021 · Tanwar et al., 2022

CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture

CNNs extract spatial features; LSTMs recognize temporal patterns. The hybrid captures emotional transitions over time, which is why the study adopted it.

Results and Discussion

What 30 participants showed

Data came from 30 participants at the Community-Based Mental Health Clinic. The system recorded heart rate, GSR, temperature and motion, plus eyebag, wrinkle and visual fatigue scores and recognized emotions. System-generated scores ranged from 0 to 100.

Findings

Each question, answered

Follow one thread from problem to finding. Pick a number, or click any row.

1

PsycheScan works as one integrated platform

The HW-827, GSR, DS18B20 and MPU6050 sensors, the ESP32, the webcam, the GUI and the CNN-LSTM model were combined and collected multimodal data from 30 participants, showing the system can serve as a supplementary screening tool for healthcare workers and community settings.

2

It runs without the cloud, on affordable hardware

Local processing on the Lenovo ThinkCentre M710Q and wireless sensor links through the ESP32 let it operate independently of cloud services while showing results in real time, which suits areas with limited internet.

3

Combining face and body improves the picture

The CNN reached 84.80% accuracy (F1 77.70%) on facial emotion; the LSTM reached 54.40% (F1 54.60%) on stress level. With facial outputs added, the LSTM rose from 37.49% to 54.40%, so the two modalities complement each other.

4

The sensors are accurate enough to rely on

All sensors recorded values in their expected ranges and correlated strongly with reference devices (r = 0.90–0.97): pulse 0.96, GSR 0.97, temperature 0.94, motion 0.90.

Conclusion

A working, offline, multimodal screening aid

Facial and physiological information together give a more complete view of stress and anxiety indicators than any single modality alone.

Built and deployed

PsycheScan combines biometric sensors, webcam facial analysis, local processing and a GUI into one platform that collected and analyzed data from 30 participants, fulfilling the goal of a supplementary screening tool for community and resource-constrained settings.

Backed by numbers

CNN 84.80% accuracy for facial emotion; LSTM 54.40% for stress level over time; sensors correlated with reference devices at r = 0.90–0.97. Locally processed monitoring can give health workers objective, real-time information.

Used with care

It is a supplementary screening and monitoring tool, not a replacement for clinical diagnosis, psychological evaluation, or instruments like the PHQ-9. Temperature, humidity, lighting, noise, movement, sensor placement and individual differences can affect its readings.

Say it"Its outputs are supportive information that may help early detection and encourage further assessment by qualified mental health professionals."
Recommendations

Where future researchers should take it

  1. DataExpand and balance the datasetA larger, more diverse population, with readings spread evenly so no participant who contributed more data biases the model. This would help the LSTM most.
  2. ValidationBetter labels for the LSTMLabels came from the system's own integrated score. Validate against standardized tools such as the PHQ-9, or ratings from qualified mental health professionals.
  3. ModelOptimize the machine learningHyperparameter tuning, regularization, feature engineering, class balancing, better multimodal fusion, and alternative deep learning architectures.
  4. HardwareHigher-quality sensorsMedical-grade or higher-precision sensors for cleaner signals, less noise, and more consistent data.
  5. FieldLarger-scale field testingSchools, workplaces, community health centers and hospitals, watching the effects of temperature, lighting, humidity, movement and sensor placement.
  6. FaceHandle a missing face properlyKeep the face in frame. When no face is detected the facial features default to zero, which can bias predictions toward higher stress.
  7. ScopeMore biomarkers and clinical validationAdd physiological biomarkers, standardized psychological tools, and clinical validation procedures to strengthen it as a decision-support platform.
