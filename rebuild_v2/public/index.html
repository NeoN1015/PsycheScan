Interpretation Guide

Minimal

Normal physiological readings and neutral facial expressions. No signs of stress or anxiety detected.

Mild

Slight increases in heart rate or skin conductance with subtle facial changes. Early signs of low-level stress.

Moderate

Noticeable physiological elevations and visible tension in facial expressions. Significant stress indicators present.

Severe

High physiological readings and clear facial distress signals. Strong indication of acute stress or anxiety requiring professional attention.

This screen is a screening summary. Final interpretation must still involve a qualified human reviewer.

System Overview (Manuscript Basis)

Research objective

Develop a real-time, portable, offline-capable mental health detection system using vital sign sensors and facial recognition, powered by a hybrid CNN-LSTM algorithm for stress and anxiety screening in low-resource communities.

Hardware platform

Wristband sensors — analog pulse (heart rate), GSR (skin conductance), DS18B20 (peripheral temperature), MPU6050 (motion RMS) — interfaced via ESP32 and transmitted to a Lenovo ThinkCentre M700 mini PC with USB webcam capture.

Biomarker thresholds (Table 1)

HR baseline 60–85 bpm; GSR resting 0.9–1.1 µS; peripheral temperature 33–35 °C with stress-related drop; motion RMS 0.03–0.05 g (artifact above 0.08 g). Outputs map to Minimal, Mild, Moderate, and Severe levels.

Team, Ethics, and Deployment

Validation and fusion

System outputs are benchmarked against PHQ-9 scores and commercial wearables. Weighted score-level fusion combines facial CNN evidence with biometric LSTM sequences (Fauzi et al., 2022).

Ethical boundary

Screening and decision-support only — not a clinical diagnosis. Data stays local (SQLite + encrypted offline storage). Participants aged 18–65 with informed consent. Compliant with RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act).
